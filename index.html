Neural networks that obey the laws of physics

A normal neural network only knows the data it has seen. A physics-informed neural network (PINN) is also trained to respect Newton's second law, so it can predict what happens next and even measure constants nobody told it. Every model on this page is training live, on an engine I wrote from scratch.

Change the physics

Measure the spring without touching it.

This time the damping μ and stiffness k are unknown. The network only gets noisy position readings. It treats μ and k as two extra trainable weights and adjusts them until its curve fits the data and obeys ẍ + μẋ + kx = 0. Going from observations back to the law that produced them is called an inverse problem.

How it works

One training step, from input to weight update. The blue path is what turns an ordinary network into a physics-informed one.

The loss is the physics

Both terms are minimised together. The first matches the measurements. The second is the equation of motion evaluated on the network itself, at check points where no data exists.

𝓛(θ) = 1/N Σ (uθ(tᵢ) − yᵢ)²
       + λ · 1/M Σ (üθ + μu̇θ + kuθ)²|tⱼ

Derivatives of the network

The physics term needs u̇ and ü of the network with respect to time. The engine pushes a second-order Taylor expansion through every layer, so one forward pass returns all three. I derived the backpropagation through that pass by hand.

a = tanh z,  s = 1 − a²
ȧ = s ż,  ä = s z̈ − 2 a s ż²

Verified, then trained

When this page loads, it compares every analytic gradient against a finite-difference estimate. Training uses Adam with a decaying learning rate in 64-bit floats, at hundreds of steps per second on a laptop CPU.

Where this idea is used

The spring is a teaching example. The same recipe works whenever data is scarce or noisy and the governing equations are known.

Navier–Stokes

Blood flow

Reconstructing velocity and pressure in arteries from sparse medical imaging.

Heat equation

Thermal design

Predicting temperature fields in chips and batteries from a few sensors.

Elasticity

Materials

Inferring stiffness and hidden defects from how a structure deforms, just like experiment 02.

Wave equation

Geophysics

Mapping underground structure from seismic recordings at the surface.
